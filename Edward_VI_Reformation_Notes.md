# Edward VI (1547–1553)

## The Edwardian Reformation: Acceleration Under the Regents

**Summary:** Edward was only ten when he became king in 1547, but his two regents accelerated the pace of Protestant reform considerably. The 1539 Act of Six Articles was repealed, priests were permitted to marry, more land was confiscated, and altars and shrines were removed from churches with stained glass smashed.

**Protestant Ideas**
- The repeal of the Six Articles and removal of altars, shrines, and stained glass represent genuine doctrinal change beyond anything sanctioned under Henry, going further than mere political continuity with the break from Rome

**Financial Motivation**
- More land was confiscated from the Church during this period, continuing the financial pattern established under Henry
- Permitting clerical marriage created "another vested interest" in the Reformation's survival, paralleling how monastic land sales had earlier created a propertied class invested in preventing a Catholic restoration

**Power**
- Edward's regents, not Edward himself, drove these changes, since the king was only ten years old — the most genuinely Protestant phase of the Reformation so far was, in practice, driven by adult political actors exercising power in a child king's name
